Nigeria’s Population Growth and the Imperative of Christian Education: Challenges, Opportunities, and a Biblical Mandate

By Rev. Chris Johnson, PhD

Abstract

Nigeria, with a 2025 population estimated at 229 million, is Africa’s demographic giant and is projected to become the world's third most populous country by 2050 (United Nations, 2024). This rapid demographic expansion, driven by a youth population where over 40% are under the age of 15 (World Bank, 2023), presents a critical juncture for national development. While this "youth bulge" offers a potential demographic dividend (Bloom et al., 2003), it concurrently strains national infrastructure and risks amplifying social instability, youth unemployment, and moral decay if not properly managed (Ogunniyi & Oyetunde, 2020). This paper argues that Christian education, understood as the holistic formation of individuals through biblical principles, is a critical and underutilized tool for national transformation. It posits that without the moral and ethical framework provided by such education, Nigeria's population growth may become a liability rather than an asset (Onaiyekan, 2012). This study synthesizes demographic data from sources like the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) and the World Bank with theological insights and educational theory to propose a strategic framework for the Nigerian Church. It concludes that a deliberate, nationwide investment in Christian education is not merely a religious concern but a national imperative for fostering godly leadership, social cohesion, and sustainable development in 21st-century Nigeria (Turaki, 2010).

Keywords: Christian Education, Population Growth, Nigeria, National Development, Youth Discipleship, Moral Formation, Demographic Dividend, Education Policy, Nation-Building.
